Singapore in 5 Days with Family

Five days, one base, one major attraction per half day. This plan is taken from our Singapore kit — it works for families with elders and children because it never tries to do everything at once.

Stay in Little India or Bugis

Little India / Bugis is the easiest base for Jain and pure vegetarian food in Singapore. Little India is the first choice — walk to pure veg and Jain kitchens. Bugis sits next to it, with good hotels and easy access.

Plan lunch and dinner around Little India on most days. Western, Chinese and food-court stalls can do plain vegetarian, but most cannot guarantee Jain preparation. Do not drop the Little India food base to save time.

Day 1 — Arrive, settle in Little India

Land at Changi, clear immigration, pick up a SIM / eSIM and go to the hotel. Rest 2 hours — do not start sightseeing tired. Evening: walk Serangoon Road, the Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple area and Mustafa Centre for essentials, then a Jain dinner in Little India. Early night.

Day 2 — Marina Bay and Gardens by the Bay

Morning: Merlion Park and the Marina Bay waterfront for photos. Afternoon: Gardens by the Bay — the Flower Dome and Cloud Forest are air-conditioned and comfortable for elders. Lunch before or after in Bugis / Little India. Evening: the Supertree Grove light show (free to watch from the grove), then dinner in Little India.

Day 3 — Sentosa and Universal Studios

Full day. Start by 9 am via VivoCity to Sentosa. Universal Studios Singapore for families who want rides and shows; elders who skip rides can enjoy Sentosa beaches, the cable car view and the S.E.A. Aquarium instead — split the group instead of forcing everyone into the park. Carry Jain snacks and water. Return for dinner in Little India / VivoCity.

Day 4 — Zoo or Bird Paradise, then culture

Morning: choose one — Singapore Zoo / River Wonders in Mandai, or Bird Paradise. Both need half a day and comfortable walking shoes. Afternoon: Chinatown and Kampong Glam (Arab Street, Sultan Mosque area) for culture and shopping, at an easy pace. Jain lunch in Little India between the two halves if timing allows.

Day 5 — Shopping, Orchard and home

Morning: Orchard Road malls or Bugis Street for shopping. Buy gifts — chocolates, perfumes, souvenirs — and check Jain / vegetarian labels on food gifts. Afternoon: pack, confirm your airport transfer, and visit Jewel Changi if your flight is in the evening — the Rain Vortex and indoor garden are free to see.

Pacing rules for elders and children

  • One major attraction per half day. Singapore heat makes a packed plan fail by Day 3.
  • Keep 1–2 pm for rest or an indoor, air-conditioned stop.
  • Carry a light umbrella and a small towel daily — short rain showers are normal.
  • Book timed-entry attractions in advance in peak season, but keep ticket types refundable where possible until the visa is confirmed.

If you have only 4 days, keep Days 1, 2 and 3 as above, then merge Days 4 and 5: choose the Zoo or culture, then shopping and Jewel on departure day. If you have a 6th day, use it as a slow day — a relaxed Sentosa beach morning, the Science Centre, a second Gardens visit, or simply shopping in Little India and a temple morning. Families with elders usually enjoy the trip more with this buffer day than with one more attraction.

Questions, answered

Where should we stay in Singapore?

Little India first, Bugis second. That is where the Jain and pure vegetarian kitchens are — a hotel anywhere else turns every meal into a trip across town.

Is 5 days enough for Singapore with family?

Yes, at the pace in this plan — one major attraction per half day. The kit adds an optional Day 6 as a buffer, which families with elders usually enjoy more than one more attraction.

How do we handle Jain food?

Eat your main meals in Little India Indian kitchens that confirm Jain preparation. In non-Indian places, use the full explanation, not just the word “Jain” — and carry Jain snacks every day.

What about the heat and rain?

Plan one major attraction per half day, rest indoors from 1–2 pm, and carry a light umbrella and a small towel — short rain showers are normal.
