Jain food in Kuala Lumpur

One chain we could verify from its own Malaysia website, and the sentence you still have to say. Vegetarian South Indian food is the start. Jain is the extra instruction.

What their own site lists

The Saravana Bhavan Malaysia branch page, checked 17 Nov 2026, lists South Indian vegetarian restaurants, including these in and next to Kuala Lumpur:

  • Brickfields — 196, Jalan Tun Sambanthan, 50470 Kuala Lumpur.
  • Bangsar — 52, Jalan Maarof, Bangsar, 59100.
  • TTDI — 13, Jalan Tun Mohd Fuad 3, Taman Tun Dr Ismail, 60000.
  • Lebuh Ampang — 26, Lebuh Ampang, City Centre, 50100.
  • Also Petaling Jaya and Shah Alam, if you are on that side of the city.

They do not publish a Jain menu

The branch page describes banana-leaf meals, dosa and thali. It does not say the kitchen is Jain. You still say: “Jain food, please. No onion, no garlic, no potato or other root vegetables. No egg. Cook it fresh, including the sambar and the chutney.”

Brickfields is the practical area: several of those branches sit there, and so do Indian grocery shops for snacks you already know.

Malay and Chinese dishes that look vegetarian can still be seasoned with shrimp paste or dried fish. If you are not in an Indian vegetarian kitchen, ask what the sauce is made of before you order.

Questions, answered

Is Saravana Bhavan Jain?

Its Malaysia site lists it as South Indian vegetarian. It does not publish a Jain menu. Ask, every time, including for sambar and chutney.

Which branch is in Brickfields?

The site lists 196, Jalan Tun Sambanthan. Confirm the door on their branch page the week you travel. Addresses change.

What if we are in a Malay restaurant?

Ask whether the sauce uses shrimp paste or dried fish, even if the board says vegetable.
