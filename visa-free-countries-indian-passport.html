Visa-free and visa-on-arrival countries

The finder is a copy of two tables on the Ministry of External Affairs site, read on 17 Nov 2026, with one correction where Thailand’s own announcement is newer. It is not a guess at a passport index.

What was copied

MEA publishes three tables for ordinary Indian passports: e-visa, visa-free, and visa on arrival. The finder includes the visa-free table and the visa-on-arrival table. It does not include the e-visa table, because an e-visa is an application, not visa-free entry.

Remarks are kept as MEA wrote them, including conditions. If a remarks cell was empty, the finder leaves it empty rather than filling a gap.

Thailand was corrected

MEA’s visa-free row still said 60 days, for tourism and short business, when we read it. The Royal Thai Embassy in New Delhi says that from 15 September 2026 the exemption for Indian ordinary passports is 30 days, and tourism only. Thailand’s government PRD says the 60-day scheme was revoked and the revised measures took effect on 15 September 2026.

The finder therefore shows 30 days for Thailand, not 60. It also drops Thailand from visa-on-arrival. MEA lists Thailand on that table with an empty remarks cell, and the Thai announcement does not offer Indian passports the visa-on-arrival scheme.

Everyone still registers on the Thailand Digital Arrival Card, preferably 3 days before arrival. MEA prints that line, and so does the Thai material. The embassy page in Doha, which includes India in the 30-day list, also says travellers should carry the equivalent of 20,000 baht per person or 40,000 baht per family.

A longer stay is a different product. The New Delhi embassy’s visa table lists a single-entry tourist visa (TR) with a stay of up to 60 days per entry and a fee of 3,000 INR. Confirm the fee on that page before you pay. It is not the visa-free rule.

Neighbours, and a waiver with an end date

MEA lists Nepal and Bhutan as visa-free with no time limit printed. It lists the Maldives as 90 days. It lists Malaysia as 30 days until 31 December 2026. Malaysia’s own foreign ministry in New Delhi adds the practical conditions: passport valid at least six months, return ticket, hotel booking, and the MDAC. Those are written up on the Malaysia page.

Singapore appears on MEA’s e-visa table, with an empty remarks cell. It is not in the visa-free finder. Do not fly assuming you can enter Singapore without a visa.

The United Arab Emirates row on the visa-on-arrival table is conditional: MEA says it is for Indian passport holders who already have a valid visa or residence permit from a listed country, including the US, UK, Schengen, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, Singapore or South Korea, and a passport with six months of validity. It is not a blanket visa on arrival for every Indian tourist.

Questions, answered

Is the finder the whole world?

It is MEA’s visa-free and visa-on-arrival tables, plus the Thailand correction. Countries that only have an e-visa, or a visa from an embassy, are not in it.

Is Thailand 60 days or 30?

30 days for tourism, from 15 September 2026, on the Thai government’s announcement. MEA’s page still showed 60 days when we read it the same day.

Do Nepal and Bhutan need a visa?

MEA prints “no limit” and no further condition in those rows. Carry the identity document the border you use actually asks for. This page does not invent that document.

Can you do the paperwork?

Visa help is priced per country on WhatsApp. The finder is free and does not file anything.
