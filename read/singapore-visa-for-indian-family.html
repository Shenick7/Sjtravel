Singapore Visa for Indian Family: How to Apply

An Indian family flying to Singapore needs one visa per person, applied for before you fly — there is no visa on arrival. The process is straightforward: through an authorised agent it costs ₹3,400 per person, takes 3–5 working days, and the visa arrives as an e-Visa.

Quick facts

  • Do Indians need a visa? Yes — an ordinary Indian passport gets no visa-free entry and no visa on arrival.
  • Cost per person: ₹3,400 via a Delhi or Mumbai agent; SGD 30 + ₹1,000 via Chennai.
  • How long it takes: 3–5 working days after your authorised agent submits the application.
  • Passport: valid at least 6 months from the day you enter Singapore.
  • Arrival card: the SG Arrival Card is free — file it within 3 days of arrival.

Do Indians need a visa for Singapore?

Yes. Indian nationals holding ordinary Indian passports must apply for a visa before travelling — there is no visa on arrival and no visa-free entry. The High Commission in New Delhi states plainly that an Indian traveller without a valid Singapore visa, who is not eligible for the 96-hour transit facility below, will not be allowed to enter. Holders of diplomatic, official and service passports are exempt.

A Singapore visa is not a promise of entry. It is a pre-entry permission: the Immigration & Checkpoints Authority (ICA) officers at the checkpoint decide whether you enter, and they can ask for proof of funds and a confirmed return ticket.

What you pay depends on which Singapore mission’s authorised agent files your application — each mission publishes its own schedule.

For a family of four through a Delhi or Mumbai agent, that is ₹13,600 in total. Through a Chennai agent the SGD 30 fee is converted by the agent — about ₹2,160 at 1 SGD ≈ ₹72, seen 24 November 2026 — plus the ₹1,000 service fee, so roughly ₹3,160 per person.

Fees are non-refundable, whatever the outcome — even if you withdraw. Only use an agent on the mission’s own authorised list: the missions warn they are not liable for extra charges from unauthorised agents.

What documents does your family need?

The list is short, and every member of the family — children included — needs their own set. File the whole family together.

  • Form 14A: completed and signed by each applicant, every column and blank filled in.
  • Passport: valid at least six months from the day you enter Singapore, with a photocopy of the biodata page and the last page.
  • Photos: two recent passport-sized colour photographs, taken within the last three months (see below).
  • Letter of Introduction (Form V39A): only if asked for, from your local contact in Singapore — a citizen or permanent resident aged 21 or over with a SingPass account. An Indian Embassy or High Commission letter can also be accepted.
  • Supporting documents, if asked: extra papers may be required — your agent may ask for bank statements or an income-tax return. Documents in Hindi must be translated into English.

A wrong photo is one of the commonest reasons a file comes back: 35 mm wide by 45 mm high, no borders, colour, plain white background, no shadows. Full face with the upper shoulders visible, eyes open, hair clear of the eyebrows and eyes. No white headgear or white clothing (religious headgear is fine if it does not hide facial features), and spectacle frames must not cover the eyes or glare.

You do not need a flight ticket to apply — but if you buy one anyway, you bear the loss if the visa is refused.

How to apply, step by step

You cannot file the application yourself. Since 2009 the missions have not accepted walk-in applications — your file goes in online, through an Authorised Visa Agent or a local contact in Singapore.

  • 1. Find the right agent list. Each mission publishes its own: Chennai lists agents in Chennai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad; New Delhi’s covers Delhi NCR, West Bengal, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and Punjab; Mumbai’s covers Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat and Odisha.
  • 2. Hand over the documents — Form 14A, photos, passport copies and supporting documents — and pay the fees.
  • 3. The agent submits online to ICA’s e-Visa system. A local contact files through ICA’s e-Service instead.
  • 4. Wait for the decision — usually 3–5 working days.
  • 5. When approved, your agent gives you a copy of your e-Visa. Check the name, passport number and dates, then print it and keep a copy on your phone. You can also check the status on ICA’s website with your reference number.

How long does a Singapore visa take — and when should you apply?

Three to five working days, counting from the day after your agent submits — weekends and public holidays do not count, and some applications take longer. Every application is considered on its own merits.

Apply at least two weeks before you fly — that leaves room for a slow file and for the arrival card, which you can only submit in the last three days. Do not book non-refundable flights or hotels until the visa is in hand. And do not confuse the visa’s validity with your stay: the validity is only the window in which you may seek entry, while how long you may stay is decided by the ICA officer at the checkpoint and written on the e-Pass emailed to you.

The SG Arrival Card — free, and not optional

The visa gets you to the airport. The SG Arrival Card gets you through immigration. Every foreign visitor must submit it online — including travellers from visa-free countries — within three days before arrival, counting the day you arrive. It is free and takes a few minutes on ICA’s e-service or the MyICA app.

Two warnings. First, copycat websites charge a fee to file the card for you. The genuine card costs nothing — if a site asks for money, close it and go to ICA directly. Second, enter a valid email address: since March 2022 there is no passport stamp — your Visit Pass (e-Pass) arrives by email, stating the stay granted and the last day you may stay. Passengers only changing planes at Changi and staying airside do not need one.

Only passing through? The 96-hour transit facility

If Singapore is a stop on the way to somewhere else, you may not need a visa at all. Under ICA’s 96-hour Visa Free Transit Facility, Indian nationals in transit to or from a third country can enter Singapore for up to 96 hours without one — if you meet every condition:

  • You are travelling to or from a third country via Singapore, and leave by air or sea on a valid onward air, ferry or cruise ticket departing within 96 hours.
  • You hold a valid visa or long-term pass for Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan, New Zealand, Switzerland, the United Kingdom or the United States, valid for at least one month from the day you enter Singapore. A Schengen visa counts only if it is good for entry to Germany or Switzerland. A single-journey visa works on the return leg, travelling directly from the issuing country home without having returned home since you used it.

The stay cannot be extended. A US or UK visa does not make a Singapore holiday visa-free: the facility is for transit only.

Questions, answered

Do Indians need a visa for Singapore?

Yes. An ordinary Indian passport needs a visa approved before you travel — no visa on arrival, no visa-free entry. Holders of diplomatic, official and service passports are exempt.

How much is a Singapore visa for an Indian family?

₹3,400 per person through an authorised agent of the New Delhi or Mumbai mission (₹2,100 fee plus ₹1,300 service) — ₹13,600 for four. Through a Chennai agent it is SGD 30 plus ₹1,000 per person, about ₹3,160 at the rate seen on 24 November 2026. Fees are non-refundable.

How long does a Singapore visa take?

Usually 3–5 working days after your authorised agent submits the application, excluding the day of submission, weekends and public holidays.

Do children need a separate Singapore visa?

Yes. Every child travels on their own passport and needs their own visa and SG Arrival Card — file the whole family together.

I have a US visa — can I visit Singapore without a Singapore visa?

Not for a holiday. A US visa (or one from seven other countries) only helps under the 96-hour Visa Free Transit Facility, when transiting to or from a third country.
