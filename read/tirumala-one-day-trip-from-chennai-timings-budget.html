Tirumala One-Day Trip from Chennai: Timings and Budget

Yes — you can leave Chennai in the morning, have darshan at Tirumala and be home the same night. But only if the darshan is already booked. Here is the day, hour by hour, with the train times and a rupee budget, checked on 21 November 2026.

PilgrimageBy Shenick Jain21 November 20266 min read

Quick facts

  • The plan: Chennai Central 06:30 → Tirupati 09:50, darshan at Tirumala, Tirupati 18:05 → Chennai 22:15. One day, no stay.
  • The one rule: book the ₹300 Special Entry Darshan first. Free darshan took 8–10 hours on 20 November 2026, by TTD's own bulletin — it does not fit a one-day trip.
  • Trains: Sapthagiri Express 16057 out and 16058 back, daily. Second sitting ₹80, chair car ₹290 each way, seen 21 November 2026.
  • Budget: about ₹660–₹740 per person in second sitting, ₹300 darshan ticket included; about ₹1,080–₹1,160 in chair car.
  • February 2027 tickets: senior-citizen quota opens 23 November at 3 PM, ₹300 tickets 24 November at 10 AM, on ttdevasthanams.ap.gov.in.
  • December warning: all Sapthagiri trains are fully cancelled 9–27 December 2026 for yard work at Renigunta.

The honest answer first

The one-day trip stands or falls on the darshan, not the train. TTD publishes the wait every morning. On 20 November 2026 its bulletin put Sarva Darshan — the free darshan, no ticket and no booking — at 8 to 10 hours without a slotted token. Two days earlier it was 6 to 8 hours; on the weekend of 15–16 November it was 15 to 18 hours. That is the queue alone, before the climb up the hill and the train home. On 19 November alone, 67,915 pilgrims had darshan.

So the rule is simple: a one-day trip from Chennai needs a booked ₹300 Special Entry Darshan (SED) slot in the late morning or early afternoon. With it, you report at your time and the darshan fits the day. Without it, do not attempt the same-day return — either stay the night in Tirupati or pick another date. The booking steps are in our Tirumala darshan tickets guide; this article is the day around the ticket.

February 2027 tickets open this week: the senior-citizen and differently-abled quota opens on Monday 23 November at 3 PM, and the ₹300 tickets open on Tuesday 24 November at 10 AM, both on TTD's official portal, ttdevasthanams.ap.gov.in. If February is your month, set an alarm — weekend and festival slots sell out within minutes.

This plan only works if your ₹300 slot is in the late morning or early afternoon. A late-evening slot means staying the night in Tirupati — the last train home leaves at 18:05, and there is no later same-day train that gets you back.

Getting to Tirupati from Chennai

The train is the backbone of this plan. The Sapthagiri Express (16057) leaves Chennai Central at 06:30 every day and reaches Tirupati at 09:50 — 151 km in 3 hours 20 minutes. Second sitting costs ₹80 and chair car ₹290, seen on 21 November 2026. The return, train 16058, leaves Tirupati at 18:05 and reaches Chennai Central at 22:15. Book both directions on IRCTC as soon as your darshan date is fixed; the trains fill up on weekends.

Two warnings. The other Sapthagiri services do not help a one-day trip: the 14:40 train from Chennai (16053) reaches Tirupati at 17:50, too late for darshan, and the 10:10 from Tirupati (16054) is for the morning after a night's stay. And Southern Railway has cancelled all four Sapthagiri services from 9 to 27 December 2026 for yard work at Renigunta — if you travel in December, check the running status first.

By road it is about 135 km: allow 3 to 4 hours by car or bus with traffic. The train is cheaper and more predictable, which is why this plan uses it.

Not starting from Chennai? Renigunta Junction, 10 km before Tirupati, is where most long-distance trains stop — the Sapthagiri calls there 09:00–09:10 — and local buses and autos connect on to Tirupati town.

Up the hill — bus or on foot

From Tirupati town, APSRTC buses run up to Tirumala every few minutes from the bus stand opposite the station. The ride takes 45–60 minutes up the ghat road. Fares are roughly ₹50–₹90 one way for an adult, depending on the bus — TTD's own pages show different figures (₹45 on one, ₹90 on another), so check the fare board at the bus stand. Buy the return at Tirupati itself if you can; return tickets are valid for three days.

Walking is the traditional way up — but it does not fit this one-day train plan. The Alipiri path has 3,550 steps over about 7.8 km and takes roughly 3–4 hours; the shorter Srivari Mettu path has 2,388 steps over about 2.1 km and takes about 1.5 hours, open 6 AM–5 PM with 6,000 tokens a day. Walkers get a Divya Darshan token on the climb, and free TTD buses run from Tirupati station to Alipiri every 30 minutes. If walking up is the point of your trip, stay the night and take the morning train home instead.

In chair car the trains cost ₹580 return, so the same day costs about ₹1,080–₹1,160. Fares seen 21 November 2026; they change. Children under 12 go free with their parents on a ₹300 ticket. The ₹300 darshan ticket is the one cost you cannot avoid — and it must be booked months ahead, not bought on the day.

Food for Jain travellers

TTD's free annaprasadam is vegetarian, and it is served in the queue complex while you wait — with milk, coffee and tea at intervals — and at the annaprasadam complex on the hill. On 19 November alone, TTD served it to 1.83 lakh people. If you are happy with temple vegetarian food, you may not need to spend anything on food all day.

Strict Jain food is a different matter. The annaprasadam menu is not published as Jain, so if you keep no onion, no garlic and no root vegetables, carry your own food — theplas, dry snacks and fruit travel well on the train — and eat before you climb and after darshan. Do not count on finding a Jain kitchen on the hill.

What to carry, and what to know before you go

Carry the same original photo ID for every pilgrim that you used at booking — the name on the ticket must match the ID — and arrive 30–45 minutes before your slotted time. Mobile phones and cameras are not allowed inside the temple, so deposit them at the counters near the queue complex. TTD asks devotees to wear traditional dress, and comfortable shoes help on the hill and in the queue.

Two dates to keep in mind. Vaikunta Ekadasi falls on 20 December 2026 and the Vaikunta Dwara stays open 20–29 December — the heaviest crowds of the year, and the same weeks the Sapthagiri trains are cancelled. Pick an ordinary weekday if you can: the queues are shortest and the trains run normally. If anything goes wrong with a booking, TTD's call centre is 155257.

When one day is not enough

Do not force the one-day plan if you only have free darshan, if you are travelling with elders or small children without a booked slot, or if your ₹300 slot is after about 4 PM. In each of those cases, stay the night in Tirupati and take the 10:10 train home the next morning (16054) — the darshan is the same, and the day stops being a race.

Questions, answered

Can I do Tirumala in one day from Chennai?

Yes — if you hold a ₹300 Special Entry Darshan slot in the late morning or early afternoon. Take the Sapthagiri Express out at 06:30, reach Tirupati at 09:50, bus up the hill, darshan, and the 18:05 train home, reaching Chennai at 22:15. Without a booked slot, do not attempt it: free darshan took 8–10 hours on 20 November 2026.

How long does free darshan take at Tirumala?

TTD's own daily bulletin put Sarva Darshan without a slotted token at 8–10 hours on 20 November 2026, 6–8 hours on 18 November, and 15–18 hours on the weekend of 15–16 November. It does not fit a one-day trip from Chennai.

Which train should I take from Chennai to Tirupati?

The Sapthagiri Express, 16057, leaves Chennai Central at 06:30 and reaches Tirupati at 09:50, daily. Second sitting is ₹80 and chair car ₹290, seen 21 November 2026. The return, 16058, leaves Tirupati at 18:05 and reaches Chennai at 22:15. All Sapthagiri services are fully cancelled 9–27 December 2026.

How much does a Tirumala one-day trip cost?

About ₹660–₹740 per person in second sitting — trains ₹160 return, buses ₹100–₹180, the ₹300 darshan ticket, and two extra laddus at ₹50 each — with food free at the annaprasadam. In chair car, about ₹1,080–₹1,160. Fares seen 21 November 2026.

When do February 2027 Tirumala tickets open?

The senior-citizen and differently-abled quota opens on Monday 23 November 2026 at 3 PM, and the ₹300 Special Entry Darshan quota opens on Tuesday 24 November at 10 AM, both on TTD's official portal, ttdevasthanams.ap.gov.in. Accommodation for Tirumala and Tirupati opens on 24 November at 3 PM.
