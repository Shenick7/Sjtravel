Cheap Domestic Return Flights India: 25 Routes in the Next 30 Days

The cheapest domestic return flight in India in the next 30 days is Delhi–Jaipur at ₹6,923 for both ways, seen 25 November 2026. The dearest is Chennai–Delhi at ₹20,848. We checked live return fares for direct flights only on India’s 25 busiest domestic routes, for travel between 25 November and 25 December 2026, and the pattern is clear: the first week of December is the cheapest window, short hops cost the least, and the route you pick matters more than the day — the gap between the cheapest and the dearest return is nearly ₹14,000.

Quick facts

  • Cheapest return: Delhi–Jaipur — ₹6,923 (out 5 December, back 12 December 2026).
  • Dearest return: Chennai–Delhi — ₹20,848 (out 1 December, back 3 December 2026).
  • Cheapest week to fly out: 1–7 December — the cheapest outbound fare falls in that week on 14 of the 25 routes.
  • Median return: ₹11,727 across the 25 routes — so half the routes cost less than that for both ways.
  • Best-value big-city hop: Mumbai–Delhi — ₹11,364 return (out 28 November, back 7 December 2026), and the fare barely moves all month.
  • All fares: direct flights only, return total, economy, per adult, seen 25 November 2026. They change daily — check live before you book.

And the wide ranges on Delhi–Pune (up to ₹24,312), Kolkata–Hyderabad (up to ₹22,177) and Delhi–Ahmedabad (up to ₹19,707) come from last-minute and peak-date fares; the cheapest totals above are for booking ahead, not for flying tomorrow.

Early December is the cheapest week to fly out

On 14 of the 25 routes, the cheapest outbound fare falls between 1 and 7 December. That is the same pattern the one-way fares showed: the first week of December is the quiet window before the Christmas and New Year rush. If your dates are flexible, leaving in that week is the single biggest saving — on Chennai–Delhi, for example, flying out on 1 December and back on 3 December gives the ₹20,848 return, while the same route later in the month runs up to ₹22,412.

The return leg matters just as much as the outbound. A cheap outbound with an expensive return is no bargain. The totals above pair the cheapest outbound with the cheapest return leg on or after that date, so each total is a real trip you could book — not two unrelated one-way fares added together. That is also why a return is not simply double the cheapest one-way fare: on Bengaluru–Mumbai, the cheapest one-way is ₹4,155, but the cheapest return is ₹9,380, because the cheapest fare each way falls on different days.

Chennai–Delhi is the dearest return — and the longest

Chennai–Delhi tops the list at ₹20,848 return, and the next two dearest are also Delhi from the south — Bengaluru–Delhi at ₹18,773 and Hyderabad–Delhi at ₹17,538. Distance sets the floor: these are the longest domestic hops in the list, around two and a half to three hours in the air, and the return total reflects that. By contrast, the two cheapest returns are both short hops under ninety minutes — Delhi–Jaipur at ₹6,923 and Mumbai–Goa at ₹7,179.

The good news is that even the dearest routes move little across the window. Chennai–Delhi’s return range is ₹20,848–₹22,412 — a spread of under ₹1,600. So if you must fly Chennai–Delhi in the next 30 days, the date matters less than on routes like Delhi–Pune, where the range runs from ₹13,984 to ₹24,312 and picking the wrong day can nearly double the price.

What the return fare does — and doesn’t — include

The totals above are the base fare for a direct return flight, one adult, economy, with the usual cabin bag. A checked bag is usually extra on the cheapest fares — budget roughly ₹1,000–₹1,500 each way if you need one. Seat selection and meals are extra too. The final price at checkout can also include a convenience fee, so the number you pay may be a few hundred rupees above the total shown here. None of that changes the ranking: the cheapest routes stay the cheapest even after adding a bag.

These are direct flights only. A connecting flight can look cheaper on some routes, but it adds hours and a change of plane, and the fares here are for the non-stop service that runs every day on all 25 routes — IndiGo, Air India, Air India Express, Akasa and SpiceJet — verified against live flight schedules on 25 November 2026.

How to use this

Find your route in the table, note the outbound date, and check the live fare for those exact dates before you book — prices move daily, and the cheapest seats sell first. If your dates are fixed and the cheapest outbound has passed, the “typical return range” tells you what to expect instead. And if you are choosing between two cities, the table is the shortcut: Delhi–Jaipur and Mumbai–Goa are the cheapest returns in the country right now, while Delhi from Chennai, Bengaluru or Hyderabad will always cost the most.

One more thing worth knowing: the return leg is where many travellers overpay without noticing. A cheap outbound can hide an expensive flight home, especially if you return on a Sunday or a Monday morning, when business travel pushes prices up. The totals above already pair the cheapest outbound with the cheapest return after it, so they show what a well-timed round trip actually costs. If your return must fall on a Sunday, expect to pay toward the top of the range — and if you can come back midweek, you will usually land near the cheapest total.

Questions, answered

Are these return fares for direct flights?

Yes. Every return total in the table is for direct (non-stop) flights both ways, economy, per adult, seen 25 November 2026. Direct flights operate every day on all 25 routes — IndiGo, Air India, Air India Express, Akasa and SpiceJet — verified against live flight schedules on 25 November 2026. A connecting flight would take longer and is not what these totals are.

What is the cheapest domestic return flight in India in the next 30 days?

Delhi–Jaipur, ₹6,923 for both ways — out 5 December (₹2,858) and back 12 December (₹4,065), direct. The next cheapest is Mumbai–Goa at ₹7,179 return.

How is the return total worked out?

Each total is the cheapest outbound fare plus the cheapest return-leg fare on or after that outbound date, both direct, inside 25 November to 25 December 2026. That makes every total a real trip you could book. Fares were seen 25 November 2026 and change daily, so check the live fare for your exact dates before you book.

Is a return always cheaper than two one-way tickets?

Not always. On these routes the return total is usually close to the sum of the two cheapest one-way fares, but the cheapest fare each way often falls on different days — so a return booked on the cheapest outbound date is not always the cheapest possible combination. If your dates are fixed, compare the return total here with two separate one-way fares for those exact dates.

Why is Chennai–Delhi the dearest return?

Distance. It is the longest hop in the list, around three hours in the air, and the same holds for Bengaluru–Delhi and Hyderabad–Delhi, the next two dearest. Short hops under ninety minutes — Delhi–Jaipur, Mumbai–Goa — are the cheapest returns in the country right now.
