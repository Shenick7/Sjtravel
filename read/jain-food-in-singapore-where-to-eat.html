Jain Food in Singapore: Where to Eat

Singapore is one of the easiest cities for a Jain family to eat well: Little India has pure-vegetarian restaurants with dedicated Jain menus, and most vegetarian kitchens understand “no onion, no garlic” without a lecture. Start in Little India — and say the full Jain requirement every time.

Jain foodBy Shenick Jain24 November 20266 min read

Quick facts

  • Where to start: Little India — four pure-vegetarian restaurants within a short walk of Little India and Farrer Park MRT stations.
  • Best Jain bet: Ananda Bhavan has a dedicated Jain menu; Shahi Maharani in Raffles City has a separate Jain menu.
  • Typical price: a thosai (dosa) in Little India is about S$4.50–7.40 (about ₹325–535); a set meal about S$10–18 (about ₹720–1,300).
  • Exchange rate: 1 SGD ≈ ₹72 mid-market, seen 24 November 2026.
  • Visa: Indians need a visa before travel — ₹3,400 per person through an authorised agent, 3–5 working days. See Singapore Visa for Indian Family: How to Apply.
  • Before you go: menus and hours change — check Google Maps and call ahead, and say the full Jain list, not just “no onion”.

Jain food in Singapore at a glance

Singapore works for a Jain family because the answer is simple: Little India. Serangoon Road holds a cluster of pure-vegetarian Indian restaurants, some running for a century, where a Jain meal is a normal request. Ananda Bhavan and Saravanaa Bhavan list Jain dishes of their own, and Gokul cooks without onion or garlic across its whole menu.

Beyond Little India, most vegetarian restaurants are not pure vegetarian: Shahi Maharani has a Jain menu inside a North Indian restaurant that also serves meat, and the Mediterranean and Peranakan kitchens mark their no-onion-no-garlic dishes one by one. Base yourself near Little India if food matters most.

Little India: the pure-veg heart of Jain eating

All four are pure vegetarian, all within a short walk of Little India MRT or Farrer Park MRT. Prices below are menu prices seen in November 2026, before GST and any service charge.

  • Ananda Bhavan — 58 Serangoon Road, Little India. Singapore’s oldest pure-vegetarian South Indian restaurant, since 1924, with a dedicated Jain Menu (no onion, no garlic, no potato or root vegetables). Plain thosai S$5.40 (about ₹390), South Indian set meal S$15.40 (about ₹1,110), North Indian set meal S$18 (about ₹1,300). Also on Syed Alwi Road.
  • Komala Vilas — 76–78 Serangoon Road, Little India. Pure-vegetarian South Indian institution since 1947, near Little India MRT. Plain dosai S$4.50 (about ₹325), rice meal about S$10–13.60 (about ₹720–980). No dedicated Jain menu was found — ask for Jain preparation, dish by dish.
  • Saravanaa Bhavan — 320 Serangoon Road, #01-12 Centrium Square, Little India (near Farrer Park MRT). The Chennai chain’s only Singapore branch, reopened in January 2026, with Jain dishes for diners who avoid onion, garlic and root vegetables. Masala dosa about S$9.50 (about ₹685), South Indian meals/thali about S$13.50 (about ₹970). Open daily 7:30am–11pm.
  • Gokul Vegetarian Restaurant — 19 Upper Dickson Road, Little India. Pure-vegetarian Indian, Chinese and Malay cooking since 2003; it uses no onion, garlic, egg or MSG, the restaurant states. That statement does not cover root vegetables — ask about potato and carrot. Vegan butter chicken about S$11 (about ₹790).

Even in Little India, say the whole requirement: a pure-vegetarian kitchen and a Jain kitchen are not the same thing. Sambar, chutneys and masala fillings often contain onion; potato is normal in South Indian cooking. Order from the Jain menu where one exists; elsewhere, ask dish by dish.

City: Raffles City and Tanjong Pagar

Shahi Maharani — #03-21B Raffles City Shopping Centre, 252 North Bridge Road (City Hall MRT). The one Jain option in the city centre — but it is a North Indian restaurant, not a pure-vegetarian one, and meat curries share the menu. Its separate Jain Menu, published on its own website, lists tarka dal, gobi muttar (cauliflower with peas), bhindi masala, vegetable Jaipuri and paneer bhurji with peas. Book it for a Jain dinner near the sights; the weekend lunch buffet is S$48++ per adult (about ₹3,460, before taxes and service charge) — ask which buffet dishes are Jain before you fill a plate.

Whole Earth — 76 Peck Seah Street, Tanjong Pagar (near Tanjong Pagar MRT). Singapore’s first plant-based Peranakan-Thai restaurant, open since 2003 and a Michelin Bib Gourmand holder since 2016. The whole menu is plant-based — no meat, fish or egg to dodge — but Peranakan and Thai cooking normally uses onion, garlic and shallots, and there is no Jain menu, so ask dish by dish. Expect about S$25–45 per person (about ₹1,800–3,240): Penang rendang (shiitake mushrooms) about S$23 (about ₹1,655), Enchanted Forest about S$25 (about ₹1,800).

Holland Village: one vegetarian table worth the trip

Original Sin — #01-62, Blk 43 Jalan Merah Saga, Chip Bee Gardens, Holland Village. Singapore’s first Mediterranean vegetarian restaurant, since 1997, and entirely vegetarian. Its own menu marks dishes “OG” (without onion and garlic), so order from the marked dishes: Magic Mushroom (baked portobello with spinach and ricotta) S$19 (about ₹1,370), mezze platter S$28 for a small plate or S$35 for a large one (about ₹2,015–2,520), pomodoro pasta S$13 (about ₹935). Two cautions: some dishes contain egg (marked “E”), and carrot and beetroot appear in several dishes — confirm root vegetables too. Lunch 11:30am–2:45pm, dinner from 6pm, daily.

Holland Village is a detour from the usual sightseeing route — go for a relaxed evening, not a quick lunch, and it suits a mixed group.

Hawker centres and food courts

Singapore’s hawker centres are the cheapest way to eat — a vegetarian hawker meal is commonly S$4–7 (about ₹290–505) — and most large centres have a vegetarian stall, often a Buddhist one marked with the Chinese character 素. Buddhist vegetarian cooking avoids onion and garlic by tradition, but it allows potato and carrot, so a Jain diner still has to ask dish by dish. Tekka Centre, right in Little India, is the easiest place to try this, with the pure-veg restaurants on Serangoon Road as your fallback.

Mall food courts work the same way: a “vegetarian” sign usually means no meat, not no onion and no garlic — ask before you pay, and walk on if it cannot answer clearly.

What to ask before you order

English works everywhere in Singapore, and Tamil works in Little India. One phrase is not enough — say the whole list, every time:

  • In English: “No onion, no garlic, no potato, no carrot, no root vegetables, please. I am Jain.”
  • In Tamil: “Vengayam vendaam, poondu vendaam, kizhangu vendaam” — no onion, no garlic, no root vegetables. (“Naan Jain” — I am Jain.)
  • Ask about the base: sambar, chutneys, curry pastes and gravies are where onion and garlic hide — at a non-Indian restaurant, ask about sauces and stocks too.
  • Ask about egg and dairy: a vegetarian dish can still contain egg — Original Sin marks egg dishes, other kitchens may not.
  • Order from the Jain menu where one exists — Ananda Bhavan, Saravanaa Bhavan and Shahi Maharani — rather than asking a busy kitchen to improvise.
  • Plan dinner early: strict Jains do not eat after sunset, and most Little India kitchens close by 10:30–11pm.

Questions, answered

Is Jain food easy to find in Singapore?

Yes — in Little India: Ananda Bhavan has a dedicated Jain menu, Saravanaa Bhavan lists Jain dishes, and Gokul cooks without onion or garlic across its menu. Outside Little India, use the Jain menu at Shahi Maharani in Raffles City, or the marked no-onion-no-garlic dishes at Original Sin in Holland Village.

Is Buddhist vegetarian food in hawker centres the same as Jain food?

No. Buddhist vegetarian stalls (marked 素) avoid onion and garlic by tradition, but allow potato and carrot, which Jain food usually excludes. Ask dish by dish.

How much does a Jain meal cost in Singapore?

A thosai in Little India is about S$4.50–7.40 (about ₹325–535) and a set meal or thali about S$10–18 (about ₹720–1,300). A sit-down Jain dinner at Shahi Maharani or Whole Earth is more — about S$25–48 per person (about ₹1,800–3,460), before GST and service charge. Check the menu when you arrive.

Do Indians need a visa for Singapore?

Yes. An ordinary Indian passport needs a visa approved before you travel — no visa on arrival. Through an authorised agent it costs ₹3,400 per person and takes 3–5 working days. See Singapore Visa for Indian Family: How to Apply.
